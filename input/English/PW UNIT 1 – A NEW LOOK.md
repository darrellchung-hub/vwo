1.2 GRAMMAR – Dynamic verbs & state verbs

What does it mean?
Dynamic verbs describe an action. Something happens or someone does something. These verbs can be used in the Present Simple and the Present Continuous.

Examples:
I work as a fashion editor.

Today I am working at a festival.

State verbs do not describe an action, but a state, feeling, opinion, possession, or knowledge. They are normally not used with -ing.

Examples of state verbs:
believe
know
like
love
want
need
understand
remember
have

Correct:
I believe you.

Incorrect:
I am believing you.

Note: some verbs can be both dynamic and state verbs. The meaning changes.

think:
I think it is good. -> state

I am thinking about it. -> dynamic

have:
I have a car. -> state

I am having lunch. -> dynamic

look:
You look tired. -> state

I am looking at the photo. -> dynamic

What does it mean?

• Pay attention to names, places, times, and facts.
• Do not try to translate every word.
• In True/False questions, check that the statement really matches what you hear.

1.5 GRAMMAR – Present Perfect Continuous & Present Perfect Simple

Present Perfect Continuous

What does it mean?
Use the Present Perfect Continuous for an action that started in the past and is still continuing or has just stopped. The focus is often on how long the action has been happening.

Form:
have/has + been + verb-ing

I/you/we/they → have been + -ing
he/she/it → has been + -ing

Examples:
I have been working here for five years.

She has been studying since 8 o’clock.

Negative:
I haven’t been working.
He hasn’t been working.

Question:
Have you been working?
Has he been working?

FOR and SINCE

for = duration
for two years
for three hours
for a long time

since = starting point
since Monday
since 2020
since 8 o’clock

Do not use state verbs in the Present Perfect Continuous.

Incorrect:
I have been knowing her for five years.

Correct:
I have known her for five years.

Present Perfect Simple

What does it mean?
Use the Present Perfect Simple for something that happened in the past and has a connection with the present. The exact time is often not important. You can also use it for experiences or results.

Form:
have/has + past participle

Examples:
I have worked.
She has worked.
They have visited London.

Negative:
I haven’t worked.
She hasn’t worked.

Question:
Have you worked?
Has she worked?

Difference:

Present Perfect Simple:
I have written three emails.
-> The focus is on the result: three emails have been written.

Present Perfect Continuous:
I have been writing emails for two hours.
-> The focus is on the activity/duration: I have been doing it for two hours.

1.8 SPEAKING – Describing a photo

What does it mean?
You should be able to describe a photo and speculate about the people in it.

You should be able to say:

• what you can see
• where people are standing
• what they are wearing
• what they are doing
• who they might be
• what they might be doing
