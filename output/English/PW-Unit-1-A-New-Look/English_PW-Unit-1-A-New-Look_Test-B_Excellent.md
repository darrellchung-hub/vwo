## 1. [T2, 4p]

Read the text.

> *For the last six months, Ava has been designing clothes for a school project about personal style. She has always cared a lot about practical clothes, but she has recently started thinking more about the materials she uses. She has chosen cotton for a casual hoodie and denim for a pair of faded jeans. The clothes are hard-wearing, and Ava believes that a good design should feel comfortable in your own skin rather than simply follow every trend.*
>
> *Her close friend Leo has a different attitude. He is fashionable and has been wearing brightly-coloured clothes for years. This week, however, he is wearing a plain waistcoat because he is helping Ava at the exhibition. Leo usually comes across as carefree, but he has been worrying about the presentation since Monday. They have already written the labels, and they have been practising their descriptions all afternoon.*

Answer in English: What is Ava's main attitude to fashion, and how is Leo's behaviour this week different from his usual style? Use two details from the text.

## 5. [T2, 3p]

Fill in the correct forms and explain in English why two verbs are not used with -ing.

I ______ (know) Leo well and I ______ (believe) that he ______ (need) a break. At the moment, he ______ (look) at the display and ______ (think) about his introduction.

________________________________________________________________________________

________________________________________________________________________________

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Source:** 1.2

Model answer: know; believe; needs; is looking; is thinking. Know, believe, and need are state verbs here, so they are normally not used with -ing. Look and think describe actions here.

**Points:** 1p for the first three correct state forms; 1p for both action forms; 1p for the explanation.

</details>

## 12. [I, 2p]

Explain why "I am having lunch" can be correct, but "I am having a car" does not fit the meaning in the unit.

________________________________________________________________________________

________________________________________________________________________________

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Source:** 1.2

Model answer: In "I am having lunch", have describes an activity, so it can be dynamic. In "I have a car", have describes possession, so it is a state verb and is normally not used with -ing.

**Points:** 1p for the activity/state distinction; 1p for linking it to the correct forms.
